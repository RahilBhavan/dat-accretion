# Framing: page and memo language

The biggest reputational risk is reading as a verdict. Applies to site/, the memo, README, PR
text, and commit messages.

- State each action's per-share effect at disclosed prices and where break-even sits. Never say
  whether a decision was right, smart, or costly.
- No price targets, forecasts, or investment language (buy, sell, undervalued, opportunity).
- Call the metric "Strategy's own net coins per share definition, applied to all three firms."
- Headlines never carry a verdict. The page h1 says what is measured, in plain words, explicitly identifying the preferred stock price for the rotations; the figures sit
  directly under it on the first screen (the dek and the break-even table), in the form "Strategy's rotation adds
  bitcoin per share while STRC trades below $X; it closed at $Y." The memo title is figures only: "At
  net mNAV m, Strategy's STRC rotation adds net sats per share while STRC trades below $X; ..."
  (decided 2026-10-08, after the reader test).
- Plain words, active voice, no adjectives on numbers, no em dashes.
- Graphics follow Tufte (decided 2026-10-13, after outside feedback): the mechanism comes before the formulas,
  drawn with toy numbers labeled "Illustration" and the filed figures beside it. Panels a reader compares share
  one scale. Bars start at zero. Firm colors appear only where firms share an axis (the map). Labels sit on the
  data. Page and memo figures use one accent and grays, with no boxes or shadows.
  Exception (decided 2026-10-14): the isometric balance in the mechanism section is an illustration, not a data
  graphic, so it keeps its framed card and shading; it is labeled "Illustration" and its numbers come from the method's formulas.
- Fallbacks get labeled on the page: levels anchored to Strategy's published figure, first-order
  values, BMNP terms taken from the offering release.
